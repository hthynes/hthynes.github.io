Methods

How I work

I choose methods around the questions I want to answer about disability, education, and inequality. My current projects draw mainly on administrative records and surveys, which I analyze in R. Much of that work involves understanding how the records were produced and checking how definitions, missing values, and data choices affect the results.

Before the model

I want to know where the variable came from.

With administrative data, that usually means reading the documentation before writing the analysis.

  • What exactly is being counted?
  • Who is missing?
  • Did the definition change?
  • What was suppressed or combined?
  • What can I actually say from this?

Reproducibility

I want to be able to rerun my own work.

I retain the original files and carry out cleaning and analysis in code, using Git to track changes so I can retrace the decisions behind a result. Along the way, I check for duplicate IDs, incorrect merges, impossible values, and missing data. I also rerun analyses under reasonable alternative definitions to understand how much the findings depend on those choices.

What I want to learn next

Methods for questions I cannot answer yet

As my questions develop, I want to build the skills to study both institutional change and young people’s experiences over time. That includes further training in longitudinal and multilevel models, event-history and sequence analysis, record linkage, and causal and quasi-experimental designs. Historical, archival, and community-engaged research would also help me investigate how today’s categories developed and how people experience the institutions represented in my datasets.

Methods

How I work with school records, surveys, and policy data

I use quantitative methods to study special education, developmental services, and inequality. Before I run a model, I check where the data came from, what the variables actually record, how missing values are handled, and whether the analysis can answer the question I am asking.

Quantitative analysis

R; regression; adjusted probabilities; complex survey analysis; state-by-year data; difference-in-differences; risk ratios; sensitivity checks.

Building and checking datasets

Combining administrative and public data; scripted cleaning; checking IDs and duplicates; documenting missing-data decisions; creating tables and figures from code.

Measurement

Choosing denominators; checking what categories actually mean; testing alternative definitions; separating what is directly recorded from what requires an assumption or estimate.

Policy research

Comparing policies across places; reading laws, regulations, and agency guidance; studying how rules are carried out; tracing how reporting categories are defined and used.

Research workflow

From source files to results I can rebuild

I use Git to track changes and write my projects so I can rerun the cleaning, checks, analysis, tables, and figures from code. I keep records of the original files and the changes I make to them, and I check for duplicate IDs, impossible values, failed merges, and other problems that could change the results.

Further training

Methods I want to learn more deeply

I want deeper training in causal inference, longitudinal and multilevel models, quasi-experimental designs, and linking records across datasets. These methods would let me study changes over time and connect decisions made at different points in a child’s experience with schools and public programs.

Methods

Methods I use in my research

I work mostly in R with school records, national survey data, and state policy data. I keep the methods here tied to projects where I have actually used them.

Complex surveys

National Survey of Children’s Health

In Who Gets Help First?, I pool the 2023 and 2024 survey files, account for the survey design, work with the multiply imputed household-poverty measures, fit regression models, and estimate adjusted probabilities.

UsedSurvey weightsRegressionAdjusted probabilitiesMultiple imputation

Administrative records

Florida special-education data

For my honors thesis, I combine 16 FLDOE workbooks, check district-year-race keys and totals, calculate program-membership rates and risk ratios, and rerun the analysis under alternative denominators, district cutoffs, and assumptions about suppressed cells.

UsedR data pipelinesRisk ratiosDistrict/year comparisonsSensitivity checks

Policy evaluation

Medicaid expansion and special education

For The Staying Power of Special Education, I use state-by-year data and a staggered difference-in-differences design to estimate changes in IDEA identification after school Medicaid expansions.

UsedState-by-year dataDifference-in-differencesPolicy codingR

Policy research

Speech and language reporting

For my federal S/LI project, I compare state and federal reporting documents, keep the original state categories visible, and document when the public record is not enough to match a state category to the federal one without guessing.

Research workflow

From source files to results I can rebuild

I use Git to track changes and write projects so I can rerun the cleaning, checks, analysis, tables, and figures from code. I keep records of the original files and the changes I make to them, and I check for duplicate IDs, impossible values, failed merges, and other problems that could change the results.

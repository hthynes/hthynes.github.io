Methods

Quantitative work with an audit trail.

I’m building a methodological toolkit for studying institutional inequality with administrative, longitudinal, and population data. I care as much about how a measure was constructed as I do about the coefficient it produces.

Quantitative analysis

R; regression modeling; marginal estimation; complex survey analysis; longitudinal and state-panel analysis; difference-in-differences; risk ratios; robustness and sensitivity analysis.

Data engineering

Administrative and public data integration; reproducible cleaning pipelines; schema and key validation; integrity checks; missing-data decisions; automated tables and figures.

Measurement

Denominator construction; classification validity; partial identification; sensitivity to alternative operationalizations; careful separation of observed and assumption-dependent quantities.

Policy & institutional analysis

Comparative policy research; statutory and regulatory analysis; implementation; administrative systems; classification architecture; stakeholder mapping.

Reproducibility

Research infrastructure is part of the research.

I use version-controlled workflows, frozen analytic specifications, source manifests, validation scripts, integrity tests, provenance records, and explicit interpretation boundaries. My goal is to make it possible to trace a claim backward—from prose, to output, to code, to source data and assumptions.

Document decisions.

Test assumptions.

Preserve provenance.

State uncertainty.

Do not outrun the design.

Where I’m headed methodologically

Advanced causal inference and quasi-experimental design, longitudinal modeling, multilevel methods, richer administrative-data linkage, and transparent workflows that make complex institutional data usable by other researchers and communities.

Methods

Quantitative research with administrative, survey, and policy data

I use quantitative methods to study institutional inequality with administrative, survey, and policy data. My workflow includes checking how variables and categories were constructed, documenting data transformations, testing assumptions, and matching the analysis to what the data can support.

Quantitative analysis

R; regression modeling; marginal estimation; complex survey analysis; state-panel analysis; difference-in-differences; risk ratios; robustness and sensitivity analysis.

Data engineering

Administrative and public data integration; reproducible cleaning pipelines; schema and key validation; integrity checks; missing-data decisions; automated tables and figures.

Measurement

Denominator construction; classification validity; sensitivity to alternative measures and definitions; careful separation of observed quantities from estimates that depend on additional assumptions.

Policy & institutional analysis

Comparative policy research; statutory and regulatory analysis; implementation; administrative systems; classification systems; stakeholder mapping.

Research workflow

From source files to reproducible results

I use Git for version control and build projects so that cleaning, validation, analysis, tables, and figures can be rerun from code. For administrative-data projects, I keep records of source files and data transformations and write checks for duplicate keys, impossible values, merge problems, and other errors that could affect the analysis.

I want deeper training in causal inference, quasi-experimental design, longitudinal and multilevel models, and working with linked administrative data.

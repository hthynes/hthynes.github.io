About

Taiel Lucile

I’m a sociology researcher at the University of Central Florida. Most of my questions are about education, disability, and what happens when institutions have to decide who needs help, what kind of help counts, and who gets access to it.

I was born in Jamaica and grew up between Jamaica and the United States. I did not come to college already knowing that I wanted to study institutions or classification. I got here by following questions that kept getting harder to answer.

I started by asking who gets help. That led me to a different question: what happens before someone ever reaches the point of receiving it? Now I study how institutions define need, classify people, and allocate resources, and where inequality enters that process. I use administrative and population data because they let me look for those patterns across schools, districts, states, and systems.

I want the work to be useful. I want another researcher to be able to inspect what I did, and I want to be careful about making claims the data cannot support. The people represented in these datasets are living with the consequences of the systems I study. I try not to lose sight of that.

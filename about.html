About

Taiel Lucile

I’m a sociology researcher at the University of Central Florida. I’m interested in the parts of education and disability systems that can look routine on paper but have real consequences: how a student gets classified, what qualifies someone for support, which distinctions survive in the data, and which ones disappear.

JamaicaUnited States

I was born in Jamaica and grew up in Jamaica and the United States. I’m currently completing a B.S. in Sociology at UCF, with minors in Cultural Anthropology and Political Science.

I like research questions that make me open the documentation and figure out what a variable actually means. A lot of my work has grown from finding that the category available in a dataset is not quite the thing I thought I was measuring. That has pushed me toward questions about classification, measurement, and the administrative decisions behind the numbers.

My current projects use state administrative records and national survey data to study special-education classification and developmental-service access. I work primarily in R, and I’m especially interested in getting better at causal inference, longitudinal methods, and working with large administrative datasets.

Outside of the individual papers, I’m trying to build a research practice that will hold up as my projects get bigger: organized code, reproducible workflows, clear documentation, and enough skepticism about a clean-looking result to check how it was produced.

About

Taiel Lucile

I’m a sociology researcher at the University of Central Florida studying how education and social institutions sort people into categories—and what those categories come to mean for access, support, and opportunity.

I was born in Jamaica and moved between Jamaica and the United States while growing up. Those moves made institutions—and the different ways they recognize, sort, and support people—concrete long before I had sociological language for them.

My research now focuses on moments when human differences become administrative categories: who gets identified, how categories are defined, what disappears when categories are aggregated, and how those decisions shape access to resources. I study those questions using special-education administrative data, national child-health surveys, and policy variation.

I care about research that communities and other scholars can actually use. That means building transparent evidence, being precise about what a design can support, and treating the people represented in a dataset as more than observations.

About

Taiel Lucile

I’m a sociology researcher at the University of Central Florida. Most of my questions are about education, disability, and what happens when institutions have to decide who needs help, what kind of help counts, and who gets access to it.

I was born in Jamaica and grew up in Jamaica and the United States. I’m currently completing a B.S. in Sociology at UCF, with minors in Cultural Anthropology and Political Science.

My research focuses on racial and socioeconomic inequality in education and disability systems. I’m particularly interested in how institutions classify students, determine eligibility, allocate services, and report the resulting data. My current projects use state administrative records and national survey data to study special-education classification and developmental-service access.

Methodologically, I’m most interested in quantitative work with administrative and longitudinal data. I use R and have worked with complex survey data, state panels, policy variation, missing data, and sensitivity analyses. I’m continuing to build training in causal inference and longitudinal methods.

Honors Undergraduate Thesis · University of Central Florida

When Speech and Language Diverge

Racial and Ethnic Patterns in Florida Speech and Language Impairment Program Membership, 2017–2025

Question

Florida reports Speech Impairment and Language Impairment separately. Federal data combine them. I ask whether racial and ethnic patterns look different when I preserve that distinction.

Design

I built a district-by-year panel covering all 67 Florida county school districts from 2017–18 through 2024–25. I compare Black and Hispanic students with White students using program-membership rates, risk ratios, district patterns, decomposition, and prespecified sensitivity analyses.

Data
Florida Department of Education administrative workbooks

Scale
16 workbooks · 1,608 district-year-race rows

Methods
R · administrative data · risk ratios · descriptive panel analysis · sensitivity analysis

Status
Analysis frozen and reproducible; manuscript in progress

Interpretive boundary: program-membership classifications are administrative outcomes. They are not direct measures of disorder prevalence, diagnostic accuracy, individual substitution between categories, discrimination, or actual service receipt.

Independent research

Who Gets Help First?

Racial and Socioeconomic Differences in the Timing of Developmental Service Entry Among U.S. Children

This project asks a question I realized I had been skipping: among children who eventually receive developmental services, who gets there earlier? I use pooled 2023–2024 National Survey of Children’s Health data to examine differences by race, ethnicity, and household poverty. The analysis uses the survey design, multiply imputed poverty data, multivariable models, marginal probabilities, and robustness checks.

Policy companion

What Federal Special-Education Data Hide

A Policy Case for Disaggregating Speech and Language Impairment

This project grew out of a problem I ran into while working with special-education data: the federal category combines speech and language impairment even when states keep them separate. I am tracing what states actually report and what gets lost when those data are forced into one federal category. The proposed reporting approach keeps the state’s original values, documents every crosswalk decision, and allows “unable to map without inference” when the public evidence does not support a clean translation.

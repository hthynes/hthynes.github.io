Taiel Lucile · they/them/él

I study how institutions shape the lives and possibilities of Black disabled children and youth.

I study how racism, ableism, patriarchy, and socioeconomic inequality shape the educational experiences and life-course trajectories of Black disabled children and youth from low-resource communities. I am interested in how schools and other institutions create and respond to disability, how young people experience and navigate those institutions, and the policies and conditions that expand agency, opportunity, access, and self-determination.

Research questions

Questions that guide my research

01How do racism, ableism, patriarchy, and socioeconomic inequality shape Black disabled students’ experiences in educational institutions?

02How do schools create disabling barriers, define need and normalcy, and respond to disabled young people and their families?

03How do institutional experiences across K–12 education shape access, belonging, and transitions into higher education, work, and adulthood?

04What policies and institutional conditions expand Black disabled young people’s agency, opportunity, access, and self-determination?

When Speech and Language Diverge

What changes when Speech Impairment and Language Impairment are analyzed separately?

Using eight years of Florida Department of Education records, I built and validated a county-level dataset spanning all 67 Florida county school districts. The study examines racial and ethnic patterns while preserving Florida’s separate SI and LI classifications.

04

About

My questions begin with what happens to children in institutions—and follow what those experiences mean for their lives.

I approach disability through a social and institutional lens. I’m interested in the barriers institutions create, the assumptions they make about normalcy, ability, independence, race, gender, and deservingness, and how young people experience and navigate those environments across the life course.

My current special-education projects examine one part of that larger story. Over time, I want to bring quantitative and longitudinal evidence into conversation with historical archives, Critical Disability Studies, and community-engaged research.

I keep coming back to a simple problem: institutions are not neutral observers of disability.

I study the educational experiences and life-course trajectories of Black disabled children and youth from low-resource communities.

What I’m trying to understand

How racism, ableism, patriarchy, and socioeconomic inequality become ordinary institutional practice—and what conditions give young people more agency over their education and lives.

A question underneath the questions

What does the record actually record?

A school dataset can tell me that a child was recorded under a disability category. That is not the same thing as giving me transparent access to the child.

It gives me evidence of an institutional encounter: someone interpreted a need, applied rules, made a decision, and entered a category into a system. That distinction shapes how I approach administrative data—and why I want archives, history, and young people’s own accounts alongside quantitative evidence.

Sociology of race, disability & institutionsOrlando · 2026

I study race, disability, and institutions.

I study how institutions define and respond to human difference, and how racism, ableism, white supremacy, and socioeconomic inequality shape the lives and opportunities of Black disabled children and youth from low-resource communities.

Research approach

I treat administrative disability categories as records of institutional decisions, not as transparent measures of disabled people.

Approach

Institutions help make difference consequential.

Schools and other institutions classify, support, discipline, and allocate resources through rules and practices shaped by histories of race, disability, language, gender, and class.

I study those processes through quantitative research while building toward historical, archival, and community-engaged work on how categories, professional knowledge, and institutional authority are made and contested.

Racial and ethnic patterns in Florida speech and language impairment program membership, 2017–2025

What disappears when two state categories become one federal category?

Florida lets me examine Speech Impairment and Language Impairment separately. Using eight years of records from all 67 county school districts, I study whether racial and ethnic patterns change when that distinction is preserved.

Intellectual trajectory

From speech-language pathology to sociology

I entered college planning to become a school-based speech-language pathologist. I wanted to work with Black and Latino children from low-resource communities.

Speech and language never really disappeared from my work. My honors thesis returns to those questions from a sociological perspective.

Quantitative sociology · education policy · inequality

I study how institutions classify people, allocate resources, and produce inequality.

I’m a sociology researcher at the University of Central Florida working at the intersection of education, disability, race, organizations, and public policy. I use administrative and population data to locate where disparities emerge inside institutional systems—and to be precise about what the evidence can and cannot establish.

Current work

When Speech and Language Diverge

My honors thesis uses eight years of Florida Department of Education administrative data across all 67 county school districts to examine racial and ethnic patterns in Speech Impairment and Language Impairment program membership separately.

The project reconstructs a district × year × race panel from 16 state workbooks, tests denominator and suppression assumptions, and treats administrative classification as an institutional outcome rather than a direct measure of disorder prevalence.

What connects the work

I care about the decisions hidden inside the denominator, category, rule, and dataset.

Across projects, I treat measurement and reproducibility as substantive parts of social inquiry. The goal is research that makes institutional processes easier to see, interrogate, and use.
